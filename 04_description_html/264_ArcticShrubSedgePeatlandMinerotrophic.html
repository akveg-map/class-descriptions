Arctic Shrub-Sedge Peatland, Minerotrophic

Overview

Level: Map Class

Code: 264

Concept: This map class encompasses minerotrophic peatlands underlain by near-surface permafrost, dominated or co-dominated by combinations of dwarf to low shrubs, hydrophytic sedges and brown mosses (i.e., non-Sphagnum mosses). Soils are saturated, circumneutral, and cold causing moderate-nutrient and moderate-productivity conditions. This type is susceptible to change in hydrothermal regime.

Vegetation

Structure

Structural Description: This map class is dominated by low shrubs and emergent sedges. Bryophytes are common and abundant, while lichens are less common but can occur in low abundances on moist microhighs.

Structural Class: low shrub, sedge emergent

Floristics

Floristics Description: This map class is dominated by Salix richardsonii in the low shrub layer; Eriophorum angustifolium and Carex aquatilis are the most common sedges that co-occur. Other common plants include dwarf shrubs such as Salix reticulata, Dryas integrifolia, and Arctous rubra, and herbs such as Polygonum viviparum, Saxifraga hirculus, and Equisetum variegatum. The most frequently occurring non-Sphagnum (i.e., brown) mosses are Tomentypnum nitens, Campylium stellatum, Limprichtia revolvens, Hamatocaulis vernicosus, Calliergon richardsonii, and Aulacomnium palustre. Common lichen species include Dactylina arctica and Flavocetraria cucullata.

Dynamics

Dynamics Description: Dominance of brown mosses represents an early stage of peat formation with comparatively thin accumulations of organic material. Where regional increase in temperature results in permafrost degradation and improved drainage, sites may transition out of wetland conditions. Conversely, retarded drainage may transition sites to aquatic habitat or where flow is decreased, a more ombrotrophic wetland.

Range

Range Description: Occurring across the Arctic bioclimatic zone, broadly associated with the Arctic Coastal Plain.

Rarity on Landscape: Common

Environment

Environment Description: This map class is a type of minerotrophic wetland that forms on upper floodplain positions and drained lake basins. Minerotrophic wetlands receive water from ground or surface water that is rich in dissolved minerals. Physiography and water chemistry distinguish minerotrophic from ombrotrophic wetland types, leading to predictable differences in species composition, productivity, and soil properties. The regular, seasonal flooding and sedimentation on upper floodplains create a more productive environment than lowlands, which are often nutrient-limited.
