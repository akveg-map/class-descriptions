Arctic Barren & Sparsely Vegetated Active Floodplain

Overview

Level: Map Class

Code: 315

Concept: This map class encompasses barren to sparsely vegetated substrates associated with riverine floodplains that experience regular and high-energy flooding. When present, vegetation is comprised of ruderal herbs. Soils are coarse-grained, mineral, and well-drained; permafrost is deep.

Floristics

Floristics Description: This map class is either barren or sparsely vegetated by ruderal and pioneer species. Characteristic species include the forbs Chamerion latifolium, Hedysarum mackenziei, Artemisia tilesii, Parnassia kotzebuei, Astragalus alpinus, Equisetum species, and the graminoids Arctagrostis latifolia, Festuca rubra, and Elymus species.

Dynamics

Dynamics Description: Across floodplains in the Arctic, both the energy of flooding and depth of seasonal thaw decrease away from the main channel. These gradients are paralleled by decreases in sediment grain size, drainage, and shrub stature and an increase in understory development. The erosion and redeposition of sediment associated with frequent and high-energy flooding in the active floodplain limits the development of soils and the establishment and growth of vegetation. Ice blocks may scour substrates during spring breakup. As this type is maintained by hydrology, any moderation of the flooding regime, for example through channel migration away from the site, will promote the growth of existing species and the establishment of bryophytes and shrubs.

Range

Range Description: Occurring throughout the Arctic bioclimatic zone, especially across the low-relief terrain of the Arctic Coastal Plain.

Rarity on Landscape: Uncommon

Environment

Environment Description: This map class is associated with the active floodplains of meandering and braided rivers. Soils are comprised of sand, gravel, and cobble, are well-drained, and free of surface organics. Owing to the high capacity of water to retain heat, permafrost tends to be deep within the active floodplain and is not a formative element.
