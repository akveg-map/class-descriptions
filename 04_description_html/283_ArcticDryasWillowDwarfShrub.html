Arctic Dryas (-Willow) Dwarf Shrub

Overview

Level: Map Class

Code: 283

Concept: This map class encompasses mesic tundra vegetation on exposed, windswept areas, dominated or co-dominated by Dryas species and dwarf willow shrubs.

Vegetation

Structure

Structural Description: This map class is dominated by dwarf, broad-leaved evergreen shrubs in the Dryas genus, often in combination with dwarf and prostrate willow shrubs. Tussocks are not present.

Floristics

Floristics Description: This map class is indicated by Dryas ajanensis, Dryas alaskensis, Dryas hookeriana, and/or Dryas integrifolia. Dryas ajanensis is more commonly found on coarse substrates or mineral soil or on acidic substrates with Salix phlebophylla or Luzula species. Dryas integrifolia tends to occur on circumneutral to alkaline substrates with Salix arctica, Salix reticulata, or Carex scirpoidea. Both species often co-occur with the prostrate willows, Salix polaris and Salix rotundifolia. Accessory forbs may include Silene acaulis, Astragalus umbellatus, Tofieldia pusilla, and Lupinus arcticus. Common lichens are Flavocetraria cucullata, Flavocetraria nivalis, Thamnolia vermicularis, Dactylina arctica, Masonhalea richardsonii, and Ochrolechia frigida. Racomitrium lanuginosum and Polytrichum juniperinum are common mosses.

Dynamics

Dynamics Description: This is a late-successional type where the development of vegetation is controlled by the climatic factors of cold winter temperatures and intense summer solar radiation and may be reset by site factors such as exposure to desiccating winds, avalanche, and colluvial processes. Permafrost is present, but ice-poor, thus perigacial processes are relatively inactive.

Range

Range Description: Occurring on floodplains throughout the Arctic bioclimatic zone.

Rarity on Landscape: Uncommon

Environment

Environment Description: The [unit_name} type occurs on exposed, windswept areas with little winter snow cover that are underlain by continuous permafrost. This type occupies upland and lowland physiography located between the coastal zone and latitudinal tree line. Dryas ajanensis-dominated types are commonly associated with uplands and ridges, as well as on acidic to neutral, organic substrates in lowlands. Sites dominated by Dryas integrifolia are more common at higher latitudes and neutral to alkaline, mineral substrates. Where species co-occur, Dryas ajanensis occupies drier, more exposed microsites and Dryas integrifolia occupies wetter, more protected microsites, such as swales or between hummocks.
