Arctic Tussock Low Shrub Tundra

Overview

Level: Map Class

Code: 274

Concept: This map class encompasses tundra landscapes underlain by near-surface permafrost and dominated or co-dominated by combinations of tussock-forming sedges and low shrubs.

Vegetation

Structure

Structural Description: This map class is dominated by an assemblage of tussock-forming sedges and low shrubs. Bryophytes and lichens are common and abundant. Tussocks are the primary structural feature of this type.

Structural Class: tussock meadow, low shrub

Floristics

Floristics Description: This map class is indicated by low shrubs and Eriophorum vaginatum tussocks. Highly constant low shrubs include Betula nana, Vaccinium uliginosum, Salix pulchra, Salix richardsonii, Rhododendron tomentosum ssp. decumbens and Alnus viridis ssp. fruticosa. Constant dwarf shrubs include Vaccinium vitis-idaea, Cassiope tetragona, Empetrum nigrum, Salix reticulata and Dryas integrifolia. Herbs, such as Carex bigelowii, Rubus chamaemorus, Polygonum bistorta ssp. plumosa, Rubus chamaemorus, Arctagrostis latifolia, and Saussurea angustifolia, are also highly constant and occur in low abundance. The most common non-vascular species are the mosses Hylocomium splendens, Aulacomnium turgidum, Dicranum elongatum, Ptilidium ciliare, Sphagnum warnstorfii, and Tomenthypnum nitens; and the lichens, Flavocetraria cucullata, Dactylina arctica, Thamnolia vermicularis, Peltigera apthosa, Cladina arbuscula-mitis, Sphaerophorus globosus, and Cetraria islandica.

Dynamics

Dynamics Description: Ice-rich permafrost is always present at these sites and thermokarst, cryoturbation, and solifluction are the most common site-level disturbances. At the landscape scale, this type forms stable communities over broad areas.

Range

Range Description: Occuring across the Arcitc bioclimatic zone, especially prevalent in the Brooks Range Foothills.

Rarity on Landscape: Ubiquitous

Environment

Environment Description: This map class occurs in upland and lowland terrain and is commonly associated with polygonal complexes but also occurs on non-patterned ground. Shallow permafrost retards the infiltration of surface water and promotes saturation at depth. A tussock growth form allows graminoids to elevate their live root mass above the level of continual saturation. Soils tend to be circumneutral, cold and poorly drained, silty mineral soils overlain by a thick organic horizon. The occurrence of low shrub tussock tundra tends to increase with increasing slope, whereas dwarf shrub tussock tundra is more associated with wetter, flatter areas on the Arctic Coastal Plain.
