Alaska-Yukon Birch Forest Mesic (Central)

Overview

Level: Map Class

Code: 111

Concept: This map class encompasses highly productive, deciduous forests indicated by open to closed canopies of Alaska paper birch (Betula neoalaskana) and deciduous shrubs that establish on mesic, well-drained substrates in upland settings of the boreal region of central interior Alaska and Yukon.

Vegetation

Structure

Structural Description: This map class is characterized by an open to closed canopy of broad-leaved deciduous trees and tall shrubs. Herbaceous species such as forbs, graminoids, and spore-bearing plants are scattered and contribute little to the understory cover. Ground cover is dominated by litter and mosses, whereas lichen cover is sparse.

Structural Class: broadleaf forest

Floristics

Floristics Description: This map class indicated and dominated by Betula neoalaskana; Picea glauca and Populus tremuloides are occasional associates. The understory is dominated by tall and low shrubs, which outcompete lower growing herbs for light and nutrients. Highly constant shrubs include Alnus alnobetula ssp. fruticosa, Salix bebbiana, Betula glandulosa, Rosa acicularis, and Rhododendron groenlandicum. Common dwarf shrubs include Vaccinium uliginosum, Vaccinium vitis-idaea, Empetrum nigrum and Linnaea borealis. The herbaceous layer is sparse and species-poor, highly constant species include Calamagrostis canadensis, Equisetum arvense, Equisetum sylvaticum, Gymnocarpium dryopteris, and Geocaulon lividum. The ground is covered in a near continuous layer of litter and mosses, such as Hylocomium splendens, Dicranum spp., Polytrichum spp., and Pleurozium schreberii occupy the bases of trees and open patches of soil.

Dynamics

Dynamics Description: This type represents an early- to mid-seral stage of forest succession and follows mesic shrubland or Populus spp. dominated woodlands and forests. Over time, and in the absence of fire, slower-growing conifers may establish and eventually become dominant. Large-scale disturbances include wildfires, mass wasting, and insect infestations. Regional fire cycles enable rapid-growing deciduous trees, which favor exposed mineral soils that remain after fire, to dominate the landscape. Summer lightning strikes are responsible for the ignition of most fires in boreal forest communities in Alaska. During extreme drought, all components of the forest canopy become highly flammable and burn readily. Mass wasting events in this class are most associated with landslides, avalanches, and bank erosion on streams and rivers.

Range

Range Description: Occurring in upland settings throughout the central boreal region of Alaska and the Yukon Territories.

Rarity on Landscape: Common

Environment

Environment Description: This type occurs on upland north-, east-, and west- facing side slopes, toe slopes, and valley bottoms. It is primarily associated with glacial deposits, loess, colluvium, or residual soils. Soils are mesic to moist, well-drained silt loams that range from acidic to circumacidic. As organic materials are slow to accumulate, organic horizons are typically thin and consist primarily of decomposing birch leaves. Permafrost is not typically present.
