Arctic Alder (-Willow) Active Floodplain

Overview

Level: Map Class

Code: 311

Concept: This map class encompasses open or closed tall shrublands dominated by Alnus alnobetula ssp. fruticosa and species of Salix occurring on the portions of riverine floodplains that experience irregular flooding or saturation. Soils are fine-grained sands and silts interbedded with organic matter. Permafrost is present at depth, but is not a formative element.

Vegetation

Structure

Structural Description: This map class is dominated by broad-leaved, deciduous low and tall shrubs. Dwarf to prostrate shrubs together with forbs, grasses, and bryophytes occupy the understory. Lichens may be present but are not abundant.

Structural Class: low shrub, tall shrub

Floristics

Floristics Description: This map class is dominated or co-dominated by the alder Alnus alnobetula ssp. fruticosa and a combination of willow species including Salix alaxensis, Salix richardsonii, Salix niphoclada, and Salix glauca. The presence of Salix alaxensis increases towards the active floodplain. Diagnostic herbaceous plants include the forbs Eurybia sibirica, Artemisia tilesii, Parnassia kotzebuei, Tanacetum bipinnatum ssp. bipinnatum, Chamerion latifolium, Astragalus alpinus, Astragalus umbellatus, Polemonium acutiflorum, the horsetails Equisetum arvense and E. variegatum, and the grass Festuca rubra. The presence of dwarf shrubs and mosses increases in settings that are either rarely flooded or seasonally saturated. Highly constant dwarf shrubs are Arctous rubra, Dryas integrifolia, and Salix reticulata, and the mosses Tomentypnum nitens, Hylocomium splendens, Aulacomnium palustre, Aulacomnium turgidum, and Campylium stellatum.

Dynamics

Dynamics Description: The Arctic Alder (-Willow) Active Floodplain experiences irregular flooding or saturation during the growing season. Across floodplains in the Arctic, both the energy of flooding and depth of seasonal thaw decrease away from the main channel. These gradients are paralleled by decreases in sediment grain size, drainage, and shrub stature and an increase in understory development. Owing to the comparatively lower tolerance to inundation and sedimentation of alder relative to willow, this type is restricted to the inactive floodplain and protected areas of the active floodplain. Here, lower energy and less frequent flooding is often accompanied by the deposition of fine-grained sediment. As the rate of organic matter deposition exceeds sedimentation, shrublands on the inactive floodplain tend to be shorter with a more open canopy structure and greater cover of bryophytes due to less frequent erosion. As this type is maintained by hydrology, any change to the frequency of flooding and duration of inundation or saturation will have commensurate effects on vegetation. For example, as the main channel migrates across the valley floor, sites can become removed from the active or inactive floodplain, eventually transitioning to upland tundra.

Range

Range Description: Occurring on floodplains throughout the Arctic bioclimatic zone.

Rarity on Landscape: Uncommon

Environment

Environment Description: This map class forms linear bands across low gradient riverine floodplains that are subject to irregular flooding or saturation. Soils are seasonally wet following overbank flooding in the spring but are otherwise mesic to dry. As the energy and frequency of flooding decrease with distance from the main channel, soils in the protected active and inactive floodplain tend to be fine-grained sands and silts interbedded with organic matter. Deposition of nutrient-rich sediment promotes the growth of a species-rich and semi-continuous understory. Permafrost is present at depth, but is not a formative element.
